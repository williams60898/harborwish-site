## Your Six Dimensions

### ① Valence: Warm

Your base color is warmth — most of the time you’re genuinely relaxed, hold no grudges, easy to be around.

### ② Energy: Low

You move slow, unhurried, with no taste for drama. Your everyday energy is dialed way down.

### ③ Agency: In Control

Your gentleness isn’t weakness — the line is yours. You draw it deliberately, and you hold it without blinking.

### ④ Social: Avoidant

You don’t reach outward. You keep to your own patch of ground, and you don’t like being intruded on or used up.

### ⑤ Time: Past

Your line comes from the past — you remember being crossed, being taken for granted, so now you guard it especially clearly.

### ⑥ Clarity: Clear

You know exactly where things stand: who’s a friend, who crossed the line, what you’ll let go of, and what you never will.

## Your Portrait

You’re the kind of person who looks completely harmless — and is quietly keeping score.

At first glance, everyone finds you easy to be around. You’re round, slow-moving, good-natured, not out to win anything, a smile for whoever shows up. Coworkers call you laid-back; friends say you’ve got no edges; even people who barely know you feel safe making a joke that runs a little far — because you “don’t look like the type who gets mad.” And most of the time you don’t. Getting mad is tiring, and most things simply aren’t worth the energy it would cost you.

But there’s a line. You’ve drawn it precisely — you just never wear it on your face. Inside the line, anything goes: *sure, fine, whatever you like.* Cross it, though — touch the thing you actually care about, step on the one boundary you keep, mistake your *easy to deal with* for *has no teeth, can be pushed around* — and you’ll make sure the other person feels, unmistakably, exactly what they just walked into.

The panda is the only creature on earth that looks like a stuffed toy and is, in fact, a bear. It can sit and chew bamboo all day wearing that cartoon face — and it can, when someone gets too close without knowing the stakes, remind them with a bear’s full strength who it has been all along. So can you. The softness is real. The line is real too. The softness is your surface; the line is your core. What you most dread is someone seeing only the first and assuming there’s no second.

> Your line was never too strict — it was only too well hidden. Kept where no one can see it, it stops being a way to show people how to reach you, and becomes just a quiet way of deciding who falls out.

## A Gentle Note

This isn’t a crisis, and nothing here needs fixing today — just one small thing worth keeping an eye on:

Notice which way your walls tend to drift. Each time someone crosses a line they couldn’t see and you move them out without a word, the space you keep gets a little smaller — and guarded in silence long enough, *I’d rather not let anyone near* can quietly stop feeling like a choice and start feeling like the only setting you have left. If you catch the circle tightening, that’s the cue to say your line out loud to the one or two who’ve earned it — before the quiet hardens into a habit you can’t feel anymore.

## The Animals Nearest You

On this map, the two animals closest to you differ from you by just one dimension each — and that one dimension decides which kind of creature you’ve become.

The Unflappable Capybara differs from you in just one dimension: time. You look back; it looks ahead. The two of you are cut from nearly the same cloth — warm, low-energy, unbothered, clear-eyed, both content to keep to yourselves rather than chase the crowd. Both of you carry a quiet strength that reads, at first glance, as softness. But the capybara faces forward: its calm is aimed at what’s coming, and it spends that calm on becoming everyone’s shore — *let it all come, I’ll hold steady.* You face backward: you drew your line exactly where you did because you remember. You were crossed once, taken for granted, treated as if *easy* meant *free* — and out of it you grew a clear, deliberate boundary: *not this time.* The capybara uses its steadiness to hold the world together; you use yours to guard the edge of your own ground. Same certainty — one turns outward to catch, the other turns inward to protect.

The Unhurried Sloth differs from you in just one dimension: agency. Its slowness happened to it; yours is chosen. The sloth is slow and warm and keeps to itself too — but the sloth is being carried: pushed by a fast world, hurried, outrun, with a quiet ache underneath of *maybe I’m just not good enough.* You’re nothing like that. Your slowness is deliberate — you’re unbothered not because you can’t summon the strength, but because most things simply don’t register on your scale. The sloth’s gentleness carries a thread of holding-something-in; yours carries the settled weight of certainty.
