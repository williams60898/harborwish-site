## Your Six Dimensions

### ① Valence: Cold

Your emotional base color is uneasy, like a wire pulled taut.

### ② Energy: High

You’re not numb — the intensity of what you feel far outruns most people’s: where others get a 3, you get a 10. That high-sensitivity radar is one of your most precious gifts, not a flaw. What needs practice was never dulling it, but bringing the blur into focus.

### ③ Agency: Carried Along

You can’t control your own reaction; your spines move before your mind does.

### ④ Social: Avoidant

You push people away — but you don’t want to, you’re driven to.

### ⑤ Time: Future

You’re forever preparing, in advance, for a hurt that hasn’t happened.

### ⑥ Clarity: Blurred

You can’t tell which dangers are real and which you dreamed up yourself.

## Your Portrait

You’re not a hard person to be around. You’re just too easily hurt. And the way you get hurt happens to be the kind that hurts other people.

This is the cruelest paradox in you: you long for closeness more than most — you want to be held, to be understood, to have one person who, when you say *I’m fine*, sees through to *I know you’re not*. Your hunger for warmth is real, fierce, almost physical.

But every time someone actually gets close, your body reacts before your mind. A sentence a shade too heavy, and your spines are already up; a message not answered in time, and you’ve already written three scripts of abandonment in your head. The other person might just be in a meeting, and you’re already rehearsing life after losing them. Your nervous system lives three steps ahead of your reality — you’re forever reacting to a disaster that hasn’t happened yet.

And then you say something barbed. Or you go suddenly cold. Or you fire off a message with an edge to it, and three seconds after sending it you regret it, but the recall button is too late. The other person freezes. Inside, you’re screaming: *it isn’t like this, I didn’t mean it, I just got scared, please don’t go, let me explain* — but what comes out of your mouth is: *suit yourself.*

> Every spine, an unspoken hug.

## If This Page Cuts Too Deep

There’s one thing that has to be said to you — gently, and in earnest.

The hedgehog’s danger hides behind how much noise it makes — you stab and regret so often that one more round reads like nothing new, just you again. So, for your own sake, watch for one turn — and watch, especially, for the reverse quiet. When the always-stabbing, always-regretting you suddenly stops stabbing, stops making a fuss, unnaturally at peace, “accepting” it — *fine, I’m just someone who hurts people, whatever* — the spines gone soft all at once, the self-attack even stopping — that’s often not you understanding at last, it can be that even the strength to stab has run out. Or when it slides into “I’m just someone who hurts everyone, I should disappear.” Real cries for help sometimes stop even saying *I’m sorry*. That kind of sudden stilling needs more watching than the fiercest self-blame.

For the hedgehog, what to watch for was never how loud the pain gets — it’s the signal going quiet: needing less, reaching for no one, caring about less and less. That kind of calm needs to be seen by someone, more than any breakdown ever would.

If you recognize this turn, remember what Harborwish says from the start: it is a mirror, not a doctor. It isn’t enough. Go find the person who can catch you — a counselor or doctor, or the one person you trust enough to tell the truth. This isn’t weakness. It’s the most clear-eyed thing you can do for yourself right now.

## The Animals Nearest You

On this map, the two animals closest to you each differ from you by just one dimension — and that one dimension decides which kind of “on edge” you are.

The Sentry Meerkat differs from you in just one dimension: clarity, and you’re each other’s mirror. You both go into defense the moment a threat is felt — the difference is whether you can see. The meerkat is clear: it straightens up, sees plainly — who it is, where it’s from, how close — before deciding how to guard. You’re blurred: touched, you curl into a ball of spikes, a shut-eyed reflex stab, hand or blade all the same. It takes the whole world into its eyes and watches; you shut the world outside your spines.

The Jittery Squirrel differs from you in just one dimension: time. You’re both blurred, both defending, both unable to stop — the difference is which way the fear points. The squirrel is looking back — startled by what already happened, that one moment in memory; you’re looking forward — afraid of the hurt not yet come, forever tensing in advance, going into defense, for something that hasn’t happened. One is startled by the past, the other chased by the future.
