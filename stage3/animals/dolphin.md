## Your Six Dimensions

### ① Valence: Warm

Your base color is bright and warm — you’re the one whose arrival lifts the mood. Not forced cheer, but a real love of watching people laugh because of you.

### ② Energy: High

You’re full of life, and can’t quite stop. Quick mind, quick reflexes, always up for it — that exuberance is a gift; you can lift the energy of a whole room.

### ③ Agency: Carried Along

You don’t scramble to lead, don’t press anyone down. Your joy is *all of us together*, not *do as I say* — you light up the room, and never once need to stand in the middle and be looked up to.

### ④ Social: Approaching

You head for the crowd by nature. The bustle is your home turf; you come close, you crack the jokes, you catch everyone’s mood — you’re the one in the group who puts everyone at ease.

### ⑤ Time: Future

You look forward, swim forward. You don’t dwell on old scores, you turn the page fast, forever heading for the next wave, the next fun thing — that forward drive keeps the gloom off you.

### ⑥ Clarity: Clear

This is the ache you tuck under all the merriment: you see too clearly. You can see, plainly — that tonight, again, you’re the one making everyone laugh, and after it breaks up, no one thinks to ask how you are. A hazier creature wouldn’t feel it; but you understand everything.

## Your Portrait

You’re probably the kind whose arrival brings the room to life. Quick mind, sweet tongue, up for anything — wherever you go, you can get people laughing, rescue a dead silence. Everyone loves the occasions you’re at, because with you there it’s easy, it’s lively, it never turns awkward. You’re a born source of cheer — and not a fake one; you genuinely enjoy watching people open up because of you.

You’re also remarkably good at catching feelings. When someone’s low, you’re the first to sense it, and then with a joke, a quip, a well-judged bit of liveliness, you fish them out of the low-pressure air. You’re like that arc leaping above the sea’s surface — light, bright, carrying everyone’s gaze upward with you. What you give this world is almost all good spirits.

But inside, you see it all clearly. You know, better than anyone: in every bit of bustle, the one making everyone laugh is always you; and when the laughter scatters and the crowd goes, few people turn back to ask, *and you — are you okay?* It’s not that you haven’t noticed the imbalance — just the opposite, you see too clearly, clearly enough to see it plainly. You just never say. Because a source of cheer isn’t supposed to have any sorrow, and a dolphin that isn’t smiling would send everyone into a panic.

So you fold your own low moods into the next leap. When you’re sad, you laugh louder; when you’re tired, you play harder. You were born with a face that looks like it’s smiling, and so even you have nearly grown used to it: handing your feelings out is easy, and taking your feelings back — handing them to some one person — has become the thing you’re least practiced at. Now and then, late at night, the bustle all spent, there’s a moment where you don’t know where to put this less-bright self.

> You’ve handed joy to everyone; there should be one person who catches the sorrow you can’t hand off. You can light up the whole room — but you don’t have to keep smiling even through the moment you fall into the water.

## A Gentle Note

This isn’t a crisis, and nothing here needs fixing today — just one small thing worth keeping an eye on:

Keep a gentle eye on the moment the laughter stops. Your gift for lifting a room is real — bringing people joy is a rare, good thing, and none of it is in question. But you see this too clearly to have missed it: in every gathering, the one doing the lifting is you, and few turn back to ask how you are. So the thing worth catching is what you do with your own low moods — whether *sad* just gets folded into a louder laugh, *tired* into a higher leap; whether handing feelings out has gotten so easy that handing them back, to one person, has become the thing you never do. That’s not a flaw — it’s just a habit of being the bright one that’s never been set down. You don’t have to stop being funny, or stop shining. Just, once, with someone you trust, try not rescuing the moment — say *I’m actually a bit tired today*, and then hold off on the joke that would smooth it over. Let yourself find out that when you go dim, they don’t leave. Being caught won’t make you any less lovable. It just lets the one who’s always lighting the room finally take a breath.

## The Animals Nearest You

On this map, the two animals closest to you differ from you by just one dimension each — and that one dimension decides which kind of creature you’ve become.

The Faithful Penguin differs from you in just one dimension: energy. You’re both carried along, both approaching, both facing forward, both clear-eyed — both warm souls who live for the people around them. The one dimension between you is energy, and it makes two frequencies of warm. The penguin is low-frequency warmth: slow, steady, even-temperatured, its love like a little stove kept always lit — not dazzling, but never out. You’re high-frequency brightness: fast, leaping, radiant, your love like that arc breaking the surface — lighting the whole scene at once, and unable to quite hold still. One keeps company like a slow, steady stream; the other lifts spirits in a burst of noise and light. Move the penguin into your slot and it lacks your springing bounce; move you into its slot and you can’t slow down — your warmth has to move to give off its heat.

The Whole-Hearted Golden Retriever differs from you in just one dimension: agency. You two live close — both bright, both loving toward people, both facing forward, both clear-eyed, both handing yourselves outward while knowing, clearly, that what comes back is less than what you gave. The one dimension is agency. The golden hands itself, actively, to one person: it strains to please, pours itself out, and its ache is *I love you more than you love me.* You spread your joy to the whole group: you don’t lead, don’t ask for anything, you just put everyone at ease, and your ache is *I’m everyone’s happiness, and no one comes to catch me.* One’s imbalance is *I give one person too much*; the other’s is *what I give everyone, no one gives back.* Both bright, both aching with clear eyes — one aims its force at a single person, the other scatters its light across everyone.
